The account isn't losing a price war. It's running out of stock.

Two years in, Nolan Interiors has built a $3.15m Amazon business on sofa covers. But 2026 revenue is tracking 15% below 2025, and almost none of that gap is explained by competitor pricing. Sellable FBA stock has fallen from roughly 24,000 units to 5,554, the top ten revenue SKUs have under one month of cover, and search impression share has halved. Q4 is eight weeks away.

FBA cover on the ten SKUs that make the most money

Weeks of sellable stock left at each SKU's own 2026 sales rate, as of 20 September 2026. The shaded portion of each swatch is the cover remaining against a 13-week Q4.

What is working

  • Average selling price has risen from $18.84 to $26.80 across the life of the account, and conversion has held up: order-item session rate is 4.76% in 2026 against 4.26% in the same months of 2025.
  • The Miracle plush range, launched in early 2025, is now the largest line at 53% of 2026 revenue and converts better than the original Magic range.
  • Branded search is dominant and defended. Nolan holds 91.6% of purchases on "nolan interior magic sofa covers", up from 76.8%.
  • Quantity-tier promotions lift average order value 39% and basket size from 2.13 to 2.97 units, at a discount cost of roughly 2% of gross.

What is breaking

  • FBA receipts in 2026 total 10,474 units against 27,798 shipped. The account is liquidating its own inventory at 2.7x the replenishment rate.
  • Non-branded purchases in the search query report fell from 136 a quarter to 54. The brand is increasingly only found by people who already know it.
  • Impression share across the top 3,000 queries halved, from 0.25% to 0.12%.
  • The price advantage is gone. On queries where Nolan earns clicks, it priced 19% below the market median in Q1 2025 and now sits at parity or slightly above.
The headline risk. Repeating Q4 2025 would need about 16,400 units, roughly 11,300 of them through FBA. There are 5,554 sellable units in the network and September receipts were 245. Without inbound shipments landing by late October, Q4 2026 is capped by stock, not by demand or by competitors.

Traffic is the constraint, not conversion

Sessions across January to August fell from 509,917 in 2025 to 376,486 in 2026, a 26% decline. Over the same period conversion improved slightly and average order value rose from $54.08 to $55.83. Revenue fell 14.9% — less than traffic fell, because the account converted the traffic it did get more efficiently.

This matters for diagnosis. A brand losing a price war loses conversion first: shoppers arrive, compare, and leave. Nolan's shoppers are still converting. Fewer of them are arriving.

Page views per session are stable

Across all three years the catalogue held roughly 1.29 to 1.30 page views per session, so shoppers are browsing the same number of variations before deciding. There is no sign of a growing "look and bounce" problem at the listing level.

Average offer count — the number of live offers across the catalogue — peaked at 325 in October 2025 and has fallen to 228. That decline tracks stockouts, not deliberate pruning.

Best sellers and the ASIN tree

Size and part mix barely moves

Seat covers are 52% of 2026 revenue, back covers 31%, chaise covers 16%. Medium is the largest size at 38%, small 35%, large 26%. The mix has been stable for two years, which makes forecasting replenishment quantities straightforward: build to the same ratios.

Pillow covers are under 0.5% of revenue in every year. They are a catalogue drag: they consume FBA slots and listing attention for almost no return.

Colour is where the shift happened

Jade white led 2024 at 26% of revenue. By 2026 vanilla leads at 27% with earl grey second at 14%, and jade white has fallen to 9%. Both of the new leaders are Miracle plush colourways.

Charcoal grey is the one colour that sells strongly across every range and every year. It is the safest colour to over-order.

FBA versus non-FBA

Requested bifurcation

Every SKU in the catalogue carries an explicit fulfilment marker: SKUs ending -FBA are Fulfilled by Amazon, the rest are merchant-fulfilled. The all-orders report confirms the same split independently through its fulfilment-channel field. The two sides of the business behave very differently, and the inventory ledger only covers one of them.

From the detail page sales and traffic reports. 2024 covers September to December only. 2026 runs to 20 September.

FBA converts roughly twice as well

In 2025 the FBA half of the catalogue converted at 9.44% units per session against 3.42% for merchant-fulfilled, on almost identical session volume. In 2026 the gap narrows to 8.04% against 4.07%, but only because FBA SKUs are increasingly out of stock, not because merchant fulfilment improved.

Average order value is close between the two — $53.96 FBA against $60.12 non-FBA in 2026 — so the difference is conversion, driven by Prime badging and delivery speed, not basket composition.

The non-FBA share is growing for the wrong reason

Non-FBA went from 24.8% of revenue in 2025 to 31.1% in 2026. That is not a strategic shift toward merchant fulfilment. It is demand leaking to the merchant listing when the FBA listing runs dry, at a materially lower conversion rate.

Of 205 merchant-fulfilled SKUs selling in 2026, 97 have an FBA twin listing ($201,194) and 108 exist only as merchant offers ($126,630).

Q4 year on year

2024 · 2025 · 2026 outlook

Q4 is the account's most important quarter and the one where the stock position bites hardest. The first two Q4s are a matter of record; the third is a planning question.

Ordered product sales and sessions from the monthly business report. AOV is sales per order item; ASP is sales per unit. Order session percentage is order items per session.

Q4 2024 — the launch quarter

$118,624 across October to December, on a catalogue that was still mostly the Magic jacquard range under a single parent. November alone was 55% of the quarter. Conversion was thin at 2.44% order items per session and average selling price was $18.84. This was a land-grab priced to buy reviews and ranking.

The best performers were the small seat covers: jade white, charcoal grey and chrome grey, in that order. Jade white alone was 26% of quarterly revenue.

Q4 2025 — the real benchmark

$679,112, up 472% year on year, on only 84% more sessions. Almost all of the growth came from conversion more than doubling to 5.57% and average selling price rising 47% to $27.72. November was $297,151, the single best month in the account's history.

By then the Miracle plush range was contributing more than a third of revenue at a materially higher price point, and the FBA share of the catalogue had risen to three quarters.

Inventory

The ledger starts on 21 March 2025, so there is no 2024 inventory data and the first three weeks of 2025 are missing. What it does cover is unambiguous: an eighteen-month, uninterrupted drawdown.

Sellable FBA units at month end, against receipts and shipments

Bars are month-end sellable balance. The two lines are units received into the network and units shipped to customers.
Month-end sellable balance is the last reported balance per SKU in each month, summed. "SKUs in stock" counts SKUs with a positive balance; "SKUs tracked" counts all SKUs with a ledger row that month.

2025 — healthy, then tightening

41,812 units received against 51,494 shipped. The gap was covered by opening stock and by 7,692 sellable customer returns. The balance peaked around 24,300 units in May and finished the year at 17,982 with 107 of 116 tracked SKUs in stock.

Inventory health was excellent. Unsellable stock was 0.2% of the closing balance, 510 units were lost and only 6 damaged. Amazon reimbursement claims are unlikely to be material.

2026 — a managed liquidation

10,474 units received against 27,798 shipped. The balance fell from 17,982 to 5,554 and SKUs in stock from 107 to 56. Receipts have been sporadic: essentially nothing in January and February, 4,463 in March, 3,715 in July, 245 in September.

At the current shipment rate of roughly 2,558 units a month, the network holds about 2.2 months of cover — and that is an average that hides the real problem, which is concentration.

59 FBA SKUs that sold in 2026 have zero sellable stock today. Between them they produced $165,198 of 2026 revenue. They include classic-seat(M)-jadewhite, classic-seat(S)-chromegrey, classic-seat(S)-jadewhite and classic-seat(S)-camelbrown — four of the Magic range's core sellers.

The non-FBA side has no ledger at all

Merchant-fulfilled SKUs carried $327,824 of 2026 revenue and appear nowhere in the inventory files. Price, average order value and discounts

All-orders report · Oct 2024 – Aug 2025

The client's stated problem is a price war. The data supports a different reading: Nolan raised prices steadily and deliberately, gave up a large price advantage, and now sits at market parity. Discounting has been modest, well targeted and genuinely profitable.

Average order value, three ways

Across the whole account, average order value rose from $43.93 in Q4 2024 to $59.97 in Q4 2025, and sits at $55.83 for January to August 2026. Average selling price per unit rose from $18.84 to $26.80.

In the all-orders report the shipped average order value is $52.49 on 2.30 units per order. FBA orders average $53.03 and merchant orders $51.66 — close enough that fulfilment choice is not driving basket size.

Promotions raise baskets, they do not buy revenue

20.1% of shipped items carried a promotion. Those orders averaged $67.90 against $48.75 for full-price orders, and 2.97 units against 2.13. Average selling price per unit was effectively identical at $22.85 against $22.91.

That is the signature of a quantity-tier promotion working exactly as intended: it moves people from buying two covers to buying three, without eroding unit price.

Shipped items only. Discount cost is the item promotion discount field, expressed as a share of gross item value before discount.

Promotion penetration and discount cost by month

Bars show the share of items carrying a promotion. The line is discount spend as a share of gross.
The quantity tiers dominate. "Percentage Off" and the GUID-named entries are coupon or deal campaigns; the free-shipping promotion is an Amazon platform promotion rather than a merchant one.
The discount programme is cheap and it works. Total discount cost across eleven months was $27,493, about 2.1% of gross. It bought a 39% lift in average order value on a fifth of orders. This is one of the few levers available for Q4 that does not require new inventory: deepening the existing Buy 6 and Buy 9 tiers moves more units per order out of the stock that exists.
The price-war claim, tested. On search queries where Nolan earned meaningful clicks, its median purchase price sat 19% below the market median in Q1 2025 — a real, visible advantage. By Q2 2026 that gap had closed to zero, with a mean of 5.9% above market. Competitors did not undercut Nolan into decline; Nolan priced up into the market and then lost the visibility that the low price had been buying. Both things can be fixed, but they are different problems with different remedies.

The footprint is narrowing

Queries where Nolan earns at least one click fell from 2,914 to 2,808 overall, but the non-branded subset fell much harder: 2,547 to 2,063, a 19% contraction. Total brand clicks across the top 3,000 queries fell 52%, from 52,156 to 24,884.

To answer the question directly: no, the brand has not introduced more keywords. Its organic and paid reach has narrowed every quarter in the data.

Dependence on the brand name is rising

Branded queries produced 58.5% of Nolan's search-report purchases in Q1 2025 and 81.6% in Q2 2026. In absolute terms non-branded purchases fell from 136 to 54, while branded purchases held roughly flat.

Branded defence is excellent and improving — impression share on the main branded term nearly doubled to 46.2%, purchase share to 91.6%. But branded search only harvests demand that already exists. It cannot grow the account.

The "miracle sofa covers" question

This was flagged as a primary keyword. It needs a careful answer, because the term behaves in two very different ways depending on whether the brand name is attached.

Search volume, total market purchases, and Nolan's shares, tracked across the five available quarters.

Branded: won, and worth defending

"nolan interior miracle sofa covers" grew from 2,677 to 2,875 searches a quarter and Nolan now takes 50.1% of impressions, 89.8% of clicks and 96.8% of purchases on it. Purchases tripled from 10 to 30. This is the single healthiest keyword in the account.

The best-selling SKUs behind it are the vanilla plush seat covers — plush-seat(M)-vanilla-FBA and plush-seat(S)-vanilla-FBA — which together produced $93,635 in 2026, at a median purchase price of $29.99.

Generic: won, but the term is dying

The unbranded "miracle sofa cover" has collapsed from 2,082 searches in Q1 2025 to 312 in Q2 2026, an 85% decline. Nolan's share of it rose the whole way — 37.6% of impressions and 71.6% of clicks in Q2 2026 — but there were zero market purchases on it that quarter.

Winning a term with no volume is not a strategy. "miracle sofa cover" should be kept as a cheap defensive exact-match, not treated as a growth keyword. The same pattern holds for generic "magic sofa covers", down from 8,050 to 2,457.

The growth question is where non-branded volume can be recovered. These are the category queries with real volume where Nolan already earns impressions but converts almost none of the demand — the gap between being seen and being bought.

On competitor counts, an honest limit. The brief asks how many competitors are already doing well on these keywords. No supplied report answers that. The search query performance report shows Nolan's share against an anonymous market total — it never names or counts the other sellers, and Nolan records too few non-branded purchases for a share-implied field size to mean anything. What the data does establish is the scale of the gap: on the two largest queries Nolan holds 0.13% and 0.07% of impressions, so the field is effectively the entire category. Getting an actual competitor count needs either Brand Analytics market basket and repeat purchase reports, or a third-party keyword tool that returns ranking ASINs per term. Both are quick to pull and worth doing before budget is committed.

Where the volume actually is

"couch covers for sofa" carries 1,223,068 searches a quarter and 11,645 market purchases. Nolan holds 0.13% of impressions and recorded one purchase. "couch cover" is another 1,026,682 searches, and Nolan's impression share there fell from 0.41% to 0.07%.

These two queries alone represent more addressable demand than every branded term combined. Recovering even 1% purchase share on those two queries alone would be worth about $11,900 a quarter at current average order value.

Where the easy conversions are

Pet and protection queries convert far better than generic ones. "couch covers for dogs" converts market clicks to purchases at 3.0% against 1.5% for plain "couch covers", and it grew 808% year on year. "waterproof couch cover" is at 3.7% and grew 203%. "couch protector from cat claws" converts at 14.9%.

Nolan's listings already claim pet-proof and stain-safe in their titles. The claim is there; the ranking is not. Nolan holds 0.03% impression share on the dog query.

The advertising report was not supplied. The brief refers to a sponsored products search term report for 2026 and a regional Google search term report. What a click is worth

Break-even cost per click is average order value multiplied by contribution margin multiplied by the conversion rate you expect from that traffic. Average order value is $55.83. Contribution margin is the one number that has to come from the client — the table shows three.

Contribution margin
Target bid is set at 70% of break-even, which leaves roughly a third of gross contribution as profit on advertised sales. Conversion rates are Nolan's own observed rates by traffic type: branded search converts at roughly a quarter, tightly-matched long-tail around 12%, category mid-tail around 8%, broad head terms around 5%.

How to spend it

  • Defend the brand name cheaply. Branded exact match is already winning at 91.6% purchase share. Bid enough to hold position, not to win an auction against nobody. Anything above about $1.50 on branded terms is paying twice for a customer who was coming anyway.
  • Put the recovered budget on pet and protection queries. They convert two to ten times better than generic couch-cover terms, they are growing, and Nolan's product claims already match the query intent. Start with "couch covers for dogs", "waterproof couch cover", "dog couch cover" and "pet couch covers for sofa".
  • Treat the head terms as a ranking investment, not a profit centre. At 5% conversion and a $0.59 target bid, "couch cover" and "couch covers for sofa" will not pay back inside the quarter. They are how organic rank is rebuilt. Budget them as such, and only run them on SKUs that are actually in stock.
  • Do not advertise anything without cover. Every dollar spent driving traffic to a SKU with under four weeks of stock converts into a stockout, a rank loss and a slower recovery.

Concentration is coastal and unremarkable

Answering the correlation question directly

The requested analysis — California buys ASINs 1 through 5, searches through this keyword, at this price, more on discount than at full price — is partially answerable and partially not, and it is worth being precise about which is which.

  • State to ASIN: yes. The table above gives the leading child ASINs per state. They are near-identical everywhere, led by B0DB6R8RDP and B0DB6RKFNW, the small jade white and charcoal grey Magic seat covers.
  • State to discount behaviour: yes. Every top-ten state shows the same pattern. Promoted orders run $62 to $75 against $47 to $51 at full price, with baskets of about three units against two. Texas and Virginia are the most promotion-responsive at 24% of items; Massachusetts the least at 16%.
  • State to search query: no. Amazon's search query performance report is national. It carries no geographic dimension at any level, so search terms cannot be attributed to states from this data. The only honest routes to that link are Brand Analytics demographics, an off-Amazon regional search dataset, or a geo-split sponsored brands campaign — none of which is in the supplied files.
  • State to Q4 2025 and 2026 quarters: not from this file. The orders data stops in August 2025.
