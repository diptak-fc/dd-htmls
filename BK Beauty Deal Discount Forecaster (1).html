What Amazon's lookback sees

How the projection works

Amazon checks the lowest price an item sold at during the lookback window and asks for its minimum discount off that price, not off retail. Single brushes sell at retail, so the ask lands exactly where Amazon states it. Sets and bundles are running evergreen discounts, so the reference price is already lower and the same ask produces a deeper cut against retail.

Projected % off retail = 1 − (1 − evergreen %) × (1 − Amazon ask %) Deal price = current price × (1 − ask). Extra depth = projected − ask, which is 0 when current price equals retail.

Example: Angie Hot & Flashy 7-piece set, retail $130, current $120 (8% evergreen). A 20% BFCM ask measured from $120 gives a $96 deal price, which is 26% off retail: 6 points deeper than the ask.

Why the windows matter

  • PBDD, Oct 6–7: 30-day window Sep 6 – Oct 5. Any evergreen price live before Oct 5 becomes the reference.
  • BFCM, Nov 19–30: 30-day window Oct 20 – Nov 18, 60-day window Sep 20 – Nov 18. Both include today's evergreen prices.
  • Stacking risk: the 60-day BFCM window also holds the PBDD deal days. If Amazon uses that price, the cut compounds: 1 − (1 − evergreen) × (1 − PBDD) × (1 − BFCM). Turn it on in the BFCM tab.
  • PED: for SKUs with no PBDD or BFCM ask. Its 30-day reference behaves the same way.

Reading the results

If the projection sits well above the brand target, either accept the deeper cut or return the set to retail early enough that the evergreen price ages out of the window. Amazon's final asks in Seller Central are the source of truth.
