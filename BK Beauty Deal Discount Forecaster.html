How the projection works

Amazon checks the lowest price an item sold at during the lookback window and asks for its minimum discount off that price, not off MSRP. Single brushes sell at MSRP, so the reference price is MSRP and the ask lands exactly where Amazon states it. Sets and bundles are running evergreen discounts from Sep 24 onward, so the reference price is already lower, and the same ask produces a deeper cut against MSRP.

Projected % off MSRP = 1 − (1 − evergreen %) × (1 − Amazon ask %) Projected deal price = current price × (1 − Amazon ask %). Extra depth = projected % − ask %, which is 0 whenever current price equals retail.

Example: Angie Hot & Flashy 7-piece set. MSRP $130, current $120 (7.7% evergreen). Amazon's 20% BFCM ask is measured from $120, so the deal price is $96.00, which is 26.2% off MSRP: 6.2 points deeper than the ask and 6.2 points past a 20% brand target.

Why the windows matter

  • PBDD (Oct 6–7): the 30-day window runs Sep 6 – Oct 5. Any evergreen price live between now and Oct 5 becomes the reference.
  • BFCM (from Nov 19): the 30-day window runs Oct 20 – Nov 18 and the 60-day window Sep 20 – Nov 18. Both include today's evergreen prices.
  • Stacking risk: the 60-day BFCM window also contains the PBDD deal price. If Amazon uses it as the reference, the BFCM cut compounds: 1 − (1 − evergreen) × (1 − PBDD) × (1 − BFCM).
  • PED: configured for SKUs with no PBDD or BFCM ask. Its 30-day reference behaves the same way.

Reading the results

If the projection is well above the brand target, the choices are to accept the deeper cut, or to return the set to MSRP early enough that the evergreen price ages out of the window. Use “Remove evergreen discounts” to see the effect instantly. The model forecasts pressure; Amazon's final asks in Seller Central are the source of truth.
