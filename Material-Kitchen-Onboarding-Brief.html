01

Who they are

Material (trading as Material Kitchen, legal entity Tokimeku, Inc.) is a New York kitchenware brand founded by Eunice Byun, a former beauty executive who is CEO and the public face of the brand, and David Nguyen. It raised a Series A and stayed deliberately small.

The founding idea was editorial rather than industrial: sell people fewer, better tools instead of the 14-piece block set nobody uses. That gave them an unusually strong press position for a company their size — Food & Wine, CNN Underscored, Forbes, Serious Eats, The New York Times, Boston Globe, The Guardian.

Their catalogue spans prepware, knives, tools, ceramics, tabletop linens and cutlery, priced from $24 potholders to a $615 mixing bowl. AOV intent is high; the assortment is curated, not deep.

02

The strategic story worth leading with

Material just did something rare: it killed its own hero product on principle.

For years the brand was known for the reBoard — a cutting board made from recycled plastic and sugarcane. As the microplastics conversation moved from fringe to mainstream, they replaced it with the MK Free Board: a sugarcane-based biopolymer blended with natural clay minerals, with no plastic at all. 100% USDA-certified biobased content, Good Design Award for the surface texture, colourways named Cloud, Shell, Ocean, Coral and Tide.

It launched around April 2026 at $48 and now sits at $52 for the large (17.5" × 12") and $44 for the Midi (14" × 9.25"). It is their site's #1 bestseller, and The Guardian named it best plastic-free board for raw meat in its 2026 test — the only non-wood board on that list.

Why this matters commercially: they cannibalised a proven SKU to get ahead of a consumer anxiety, and the trade press validated the call within months. That is a founder who will take a strategic argument seriously — which is the register to pitch in.

03

Paid media: the tank is empty

This is the headline finding, and the single most useful thing to walk in with.

Gap

Zero active Meta ads, anywhere

Searched the Ad Library against their verified Page (@materialkitchen) with Active status, All ads, all countries: no results. Their only historical records are seven items from Nov 2021 – Jun 2022, one of which was removed for an Advertising Standards breach and one for a missing disclaimer. A brand with 93K Instagram followers and a product The Guardian just endorsed is running no prospecting, no retargeting, no Advantage+ catalogue.

Gap

No Google brand defence

The SERP for "material kitchen" returns no ad block and no Shopping unit. Their organic result is strong — sitelinks to All Products, Cookware, MK Free Board, Tools, Knives — but any competitor could buy above it tomorrow for pennies.

Nuance

Their brand name is a keyword liability

"Material kitchen" collides with kitchen-cabinet-material and modular-kitchen searches — the SERP is half YouTube videos about MDF versus plywood. Any brand-volume number they've been quoted is inflated with non-branded, non-buying intent. Their real branded terms are reBoard, MK Free Board and Material Kitchen knives.

Working

Amazon Sponsored Products is live and holding

On a branded Amazon search they own SP position 1 and 6, plus organic 1, 2 and 3. So someone is running Amazon PPC — it's the only paid channel switched on.

Gap

Competitors are conquesting their own brand term

SP slots 2, 3, 4 and 5 on "material kitchen" go to Classic Series, Essential Series, Epicurean and a silicone utensil brand. There is no Sponsored Brands headline and no Brand Store banner defending the top of that page — the cheapest fix in the account.

05

Category trends, on and off Amazon

On Amazon: the non-toxic board war is being won on price

The cutting board category has reorganised itself around "what is this made of". The volume leaders are all cheap material stories: titanium boards at $25–$30 doing 10,000+ units a month and holding #1 Best Seller, stainless steel at $18–$20 doing 7,000–8,000, wheat-straw and wood-fibre composites at $20–$25. Legacy incumbents sit on enormous review moats — Gorilla Grip 52K ratings, SMIRLY 14K, Epicurean 7K.

Material is playing the same "non-toxic" hand at 2× the category price with 1% of the review volume. Their defensible angles are the ones the mass players cannot claim: USDA-certified biobased, knife-friendly (titanium's known weakness is dulling blades), and third-party editorial validation. None of that is currently in their listing copy or A+ content.

Off Amazon: the demand is editorial, not paid

The category's traffic is being routed by publishers, not advertisers — The Guardian's 2026 plastic-free board test, EatingWell's non-toxic round-up, Food & Wine's Material-specific review, plus a large Reddit corpus in r/cookware and r/Cuttingboards where "non toxic cutting board" is a recurring thread. Material already wins in that layer. What they don't do is convert it: no paid search capturing the traffic those articles create, and no retargeting on the people who read them.

The social commerce number to bring up: TikTok Shop is forecast at roughly $23.4B in US retail ecommerce for 2026 and about 22.8% of US social commerce, with ~35% of US adults having bought through it. Home and kitchen is one of its expansion categories. Material has 3,411 TikTok followers and no shop.

07

Six openers for the first call

Each one is a specific, checkable observation rather than a pitch — designed so they do the talking.

"The Guardian just named the MK Free Board best plastic-free board for raw meat — and you're not running a single ad anywhere to capitalise on it. Was that a deliberate pause?"

Leads with a compliment they've earned, lands the zero-ads finding without sounding like an audit, and the question is genuinely open — the answer (cash, agency churn, tariffs, a founder who distrusts paid) tells you what kind of engagement this is.

"Your Amazon spec table currently lists the board's material as polycarbonate. Given the whole product exists to escape plastic, that one field is probably costing you conversions."

Highly specific, verifiable in ten seconds while you're on the call, and demonstrates you actually looked. Nothing builds credibility with an Amazon-literate founder faster than a real listing defect.

"Your reviewers keep asking for a smaller board. You already sell the Midi at $44 on your own site — it just isn't on Amazon."

Frames you as revenue-additive rather than fee-additive in the first five minutes, and it's a change they can make without you, which paradoxically makes them more likely to hire you.

"Every knife SKU in your Amazon store reads 'currently unavailable', and a lot of the knives on your own site are on preorder. Is that a supply issue or a deliberate wind-down?"

Shows you looked at both channels and connected them. It's also the question that surfaces their real constraint — if it's supply, no amount of media spend helps, and saying so earns enormous trust.

"Your 'wooden boards aren't really it' TikTok did about 130K views off a 3,400-follower account. That's the whole thesis of the brand, and it's the only place you've said it in that format."

Uses their own best-performing content as the evidence, which makes the social-commerce recommendation feel like their idea. Pairs naturally with the TikTok Shop sizing.

"You killed the reBoard — a proven bestseller — because of where the microplastics conversation was heading. Most brands wouldn't have. What did the internal argument look like?"

Pure relationship-building, no agenda. It flatters a real decision, gets the founder talking about strategy rather than tactics, and tells you how they weigh conviction against revenue — which is exactly what you need to know before proposing anything.

What to verify before you commit to numbers

  • Meta Ad Library shows active non-political ads only outside the EU/UK. "Zero active" is solid for today; it is not proof they never ran ads in 2023–2025.
  • The Google category SERP was geo-served to India from the browser used. The brand-term finding (no US ad unit) is reliable; treat the category ad landscape as indicative only until re-run on a US IP.
  • Search volumes are unverified. Nothing here comes from Keyword Planner or Helium 10 — get real branded and non-branded volumes before any forecast, especially given the brand-name collision.
  • Crunchbase lists 1–10 employees, which is almost certainly stale. Confirm team size and who currently owns paid before scoping.
  • Amazon inventory and Prime status change daily. Re-check the stock counts on the morning of the call so you're quoting live numbers.
